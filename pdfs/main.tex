We apply Ant Colony Optimization to the maximum $k$-defective edge biclique problem, an NP-hard optimization. We compare its performance to other proposed heuristics and find it complements previously proposed heuristics to serve as an upper bound for . We find that our algorithm offers a promising ability to identify $k$-MDB with lower dependence on a tuned hyperparameter, $\theta$, than previous approaches. We evaluate our approach on several small and large bipartite graphs, and identify several opportunities to further increase performance.

\section{Introduction}

A bipartite graph is a common graph structure where one set of vertices is connected solely to another. Bipartite graphs can model user-product behavior, person-place interaction, and more. A biclique is a set of vertices on a bipartite graph such that each vertex on one side is connected to each vertex on the other.  The maximum edge biclique problem aims to identify the biclique with the most edges on a bipartite graph.  The problem has applications in online recommendation systems, fraud detection, and community detection [1].

Real-world data often introduces noise to graphs, and structures with relatively few missing connections may still be relevant. A biclique that is allowed up to $k$ missing edges, or $k$-defective biclique, can overcome this real-world noise. Identifying the maximum $k$-defective edge biclique ($k$-MDB) remains an emerging field.

The $k$-MDB problem is NP-hard. Unless P = NP, there is no algorithm guaranteed to find the maximum solution that can run in polynomial time. When the absolute optimum is not necessary, approximation techniques are useful to significantly reduce computation while providing meaningfully large bicliques.

Dorigo et al. first proposed Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) as a metaheuristic to solve difficult combinatorial optimization problems, specifically the Traveling Salesman Problem [2]. Several improvements have been proposed to the algorithm since its inception, most notably the Min-Max Ant System (MMAS) [4]. We adapt MMAS as a heuristic approach to identifying the $k$-MDB. We observe it performs significantly faster than brute-force algorithms while still returning comparably large $k$-defective bicliques.

\section{Background}

\subsection{Definitions}

Define a bipartite graph as a graph $G = (U, V, E)$, where $U$ and $V$ are two disjoint sets of vertices, and $E \subseteq U \times V$ is the set of edges between $U$ and $V$. Define $n = |U| + |V|$ and $m = |E|$. Let $S = (U_S, V_S)$ denote a subset of vertices where $U_S \subseteq U$ and $V_S \subseteq V$. Let $G(S) = (U_S, V_S, E_S)$ be the induced subgraph of $S$ where $E_S = \{(u, v) \mid u \in U_S \wedge v \in V_S \wedge (u, v) \in E \}$. Define $\bar{E}(S) = \{(u, v) \mid u \in U_S \wedge v \in V_S \wedge (u, v) \not\in E \}$ be the set of non-edges in a subgraph. Let a $k$-defective biclique be a subgraph $S$ where $|\bar{E}(S)| \leq k$.

Define the neighbors of a node $u$ in a subgraph $S$ as $N_S(u) = \{v \in S \mid \{u, v\} \in E(S)\}$. Define the nonneighbors of a node as $\bar{N}_S(u) = \{v \in S \mid (u, v) \not\in E(S)\}$. Define the degree of a node as $d_S(u) = |N_S(u)|$, and the nondegree as $\bar{d}_S(u) = |\bar{N}_S(u)|$.

Define a maximal $k$-MDB as a subgraph $S$ that cannot be expanded. That is, for any node $u \not\in S$, $|\bar{E}(S \cup \{u\})| > k$. Define the maximum $k$-MDB as the largest maximal $k$-MDB in a graph $G$. Let $\theta$ be a user-defined limitation on the solution space. The algorithms proposed in this paper seek to find the maximum $k$-MDB where both sides have at least $\theta$ vertices. This is because, on real-world graphs, $k$-defective bicliques can be formed with many edges but disproportionate vertex counts, where one side has very few vertices while the other has many [1]. Without the $\theta$ term, the utility of the algorithm is limited.

\subsection{Computational Complexity}

Finding the maximum $k$-MDB on a bipartite graph is an NP-hard optimization problem. The problem reduces to the well-known NP-hard maximum clique problem, which seeks to find the maximum set of vertices on a graph connected to every other vertex [1].

\subsection{Previous Work}

\section{Methods}

ACO traditionally has a set of ants that traverse a graph, leaving pheromone that attracts other ants to follow their path. As the ants explore the solution space, they all converge on one particular path as the quantity pheromones on that path becomes increasingly larger.

We adapt the ACO algorithm to the $k$-MDB problem. A population of ants iteratively adds vertices from $G$ to a subgraph $S$, preferring nodes with higher $d_S$ and higher pheromone $\tau$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{ACO}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\begin{algorithmic}
    \Ensure a subset of vertices $S$ such that $G(S)$ is a $k$-MDB

    \Function{ACO}{$G$, $N_e$, $N_g$, $E$, $T$}
        \ForAll{$u \in G$}
            \State $\tau(u) \gets 1$
        \EndFor
        \State $e \gets 0$

        \State $B \gets \{\}$
        
        \While{$e < N_e$}
            \State $e \gets e + 1$
            \State $A \gets$ $N_a$ empty subgraphs
            \State $\tau_C \gets \tau$
            
            \ForPar{$S_i \in S$}
                \While{$|\bar{E}(S_i)|<k$}
                    \State $next \gets \text{softmax}$ nodes in $\Call{Candidate}{S_i}$ based on  $\Call{desirability}{u, S_i, \tau_C}$
                    \State add $next$ to $S_i$
                    \State $\tau_C(u) \gets \tau_C(u) + T$
                \EndWhile
            \EndForPar

            \State $S_B \gets \text{argmax}_{S_i \in S}{\Call{InstanceFitness}{S_i}}$

            \If{$\Call{InstanceFitness}{S_B} > \Call{InstanceFitness}{B}$}
                \State $B \gets S_B$
            \EndIf

            \State $\tau \gets \tau_C$

            \ForAll{$u \in G$}
                \State $\tau(u) \gets \tau(u) \times E$
            \EndFor
        \EndWhile
        
        \State \Return $B$
    \EndFunction

    \Function{Candidate}{$S$}
        \State \Return $\{u \in G \mid k \geq |\bar{E}(S \cup \{u\})|\}$
    \EndFunction
    
    \Function{Desirability}{$u, S, \tau$}
        \State $\eta \gets d_S(u) + d_G(u) \times \sigma(|U_S| + |V_S|)$
        \State \Return $\tau(u) \times \eta^3$
    \EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{minipage}
\end{algorithm}

We also propose several additions to this algorithm that empirically improve performance. We introduce an elitist-style selection process that deposits pheromones on the best solutions, as proposed by [Schiff]. To increase ant diversity, we softmax the solutions ranked by their fitness. We define the fitness function below.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Instance Fitness}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\begin{algorithmic}
    \Function{InstanceFitness}{$S = (U_S, V_S)$}
        \State $m \gets 1$
        \If{$\min(|U_S|, |V_S|) \geq \theta$}
            \State $m \gets \max{|U_S, V_S|}$
        \EndIf
        \State \Return $m \times \min(|U_S|, |V_S|)^2$
    \EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{minipage}
\end{algorithm}

Additionally, to encourage bicliques with at least $\theta$ vertices on both sides, we limit ants to exploring vertices on the side with fewer in their current subgraph if at least one side does not have $\theta$ vertices.

Finally, we implement the Min-Max ACO system.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Repair}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\begin{algorithmic}
    \Function{Repair}{$G$, $S$}
        \State $S_T \gets$ tabu repair of $S$
        \State $S_G \gets$ $S$
        \While{$|\bar{E}(S_G)| < k$}
            \State $C \gets \{u \in G \setminus S_G \mid k \geq |\bar{E}(S_G \cup \{u\})|\}$

            \If{$|C|=0$}
                \State break
            \EndIf
            
            \State append $\text{argmax}_{u \in G \setminus S_G}{d_{S_G}u}$
        \EndWhile

        \State \Return $\text{argmax}_{\{S \in S_T, S_G\}}{\Call{InstanceFitness}{S}}$
    \EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{minipage}
\end{algorithm}

Finally, we implement the approach proposed by [Cui et al.]. [Cui et al.] observe that many $k$-MDB have one side with exactly $\theta$ vertices and propose a heuristic that removes nodes from one side while adding nodes to another until there are $\theta$ nodes on that side. We summarize their algorithm below.

\
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Cui Heuristic}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\begin{algorithmic}

    \Ensure a $k$-defective biclique of $G$

    \Function{CuiHeuristic}{$G$}
        \State $U\gets \{\}$
        \State $V \gets V_G$

        \While{$|U| < \theta$}
            \State append $u \in U_G \setminus U$ with largest $d_V$ to $U$
            \While{$|\bar{E}(U \cup V)| > k$}
                \State remove $v \in V$ with largest $\bar{d}_U$ from $V$
            \EndWhile
        \EndWhile

        \State \Return $U \cup V$
    \EndFunction
    
\end{algorithmic}
\end{minipage}
\end{algorithm}

They then propose the following branch-and-pivot algorithm that takes advantage of this initial heuristic upper bound. The algorithm checks combinations of $S = (U_S, V_S)$ by maintaining a candidate set $C = (U_C, V_C)$ of all remaining nodes $u \in G \mathrel{\text{s.t.}} |\bar{E}(S \cup \{u\})| \leq k$ that could still be added to $S$. It recursively branches on $(S^\prime = S \cup \{u \in C\}, C^\prime = \text{candidate set of } S^\prime)$ and $(S^\prime = S, C^\prime = C \setminus \{u \in C\})$. Several optimizations are added to make this algorithm faster than a naive brute-force approach. Its time complexity is $O(m\beta_k^n)$, where $\beta$ is the largest real root of the equation $x^{2k + 5} - 2x^{2k + 4} +  x^{k + 3} - 2x + 2 = 0$. 

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Branch and Pivot}
\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}
\begin{algorithmic}
\State $D^* \gets \Call{CuiHeuristic}{G}$
\Function{BranchP}{$S = (U_S, V_S), C = (U_C, V_C)$}
        \If{$C = \varnothing$}
            \If{$|E(S)| > |E(D^*)|$ and $|U_S| \geq \theta$ and $|V_S| \geq \theta$}
                \State $D^* \gets S$
                \State \Return
            \EndIf
        \EndIf

        \State $C_0 \gets \{v \in C \mid \bar{d}_S(v) = 0\}$
        \If{$C_0 = \varnothing$ or $|C \setminus C_0| \geq k - |\bar{E}(S)|$}
            \State $u \gets \text{argmax}_{v \in C}{\bar{d}_S}$
            \State $(S^\prime, C^\prime) \gets \Call{Update}{S, C, u}$
            \State \Call{BranchP}{$S^\prime \cup \{u\}, C^\prime$}
            \State \Call{BranchP}{$S, C \setminus \{u\}$}
        \Else
            \State $u \gets \text{argmin}_{v \in C_0}\bar{d}_C$
            \If{$\bar{d}_C(u) > k - |\bar{E}(S)| > 0$}
                \State $(S^\prime, C^\prime) \gets \Call{Update}{S, C, u}$
                \State \Call{BranchP}{$S^\prime \cup \{u\}, C^\prime$}
                \State \Call{BranchP}{$S, C \setminus \{u\}$}
            \Else
                \For{$v \in \{u\} \cup N_C(u)$}
                    \State $(S^\prime, C^\prime) \gets \Call{Update}{S, C, v}$
                    \State \Call{BranchP}{$S^\prime \cup \{v\}, C^\prime$}
                    \State $C \gets C \setminus \{v\}$
                \EndFor
            \EndIf
        \EndIf
    \EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{minipage}
\end{algorithm}

Where $D^*$ is the $k$-MDB of the graph.

The paper proposes several novel optimization techniques as well. These techniques are beyond the scope of this paper but were implemented during testing. These optimizations include several graph reduction techniques that remove nodes provably not part of a $k$-MDB.
